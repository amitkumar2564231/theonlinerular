Frequently asked

Why do I need to calibrate at all?–
Browsers don't expose your screen's real pixel density, so any on-screen ruler that skips calibration is just assuming a number — usually 96 pixels per inch — which is wrong for most modern phones, laptops, and monitors. Matching a known object once tells the ruler the truth for your specific device.
Which calibration method is more accurate?+
Matching a physical card, bill, or sheet of paper directly against your screen is the most reliable, since it measures your actual rendering rather than a manufacturer's spec sheet. Entering your screen's resolution and diagonal is a reasonable fallback, but it assumes your panel's stated size and resolution are exact.
Does this work if I zoom my browser in or out?+
No — zooming changes how many physical pixels make up a CSS pixel, which throws off the calibration. If you change your zoom level, recalibrate to keep measurements accurate.
Is my calibration data sent anywhere?+
No. The calibration value is stored only in your browser, on your own device, and is never transmitted anywhere.
Why is there a separate printable ruler?+
A printed ruler doesn't depend on screen calibration at all — it relies on your printer rendering the page at true size, which is generally more reliable for precise measurements once it's on paper.
